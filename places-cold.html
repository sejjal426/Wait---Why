Why Are Some Places So Cold?

Why can one place be covered in snow while another place at the same time feels warm and sunny? The answer isn't simply "because it is closer to the poles." Several different factors work together to control how cold a place becomes.

☀️ It Starts With the Sun

The Sun is Earth's main source of heat. But sunlight does not hit every part of Earth at the same angle.

Near the equator, sunlight generally arrives more directly. Near the poles, sunlight arrives at a much lower angle and is spread over a larger area.

The same amount of sunlight can heat a smaller area more strongly when it arrives more directly.

🌍 Latitude Makes a Difference

Latitude tells us how far a place is north or south of the equator.

Places at higher latitudes usually receive less direct solar energy, especially during their winter months. This is one major reason polar regions are so cold.

🏔️ Mountains Can Make Places Colder

Height above sea level also affects temperature. As you travel higher into the atmosphere, air pressure decreases and the air generally becomes colder.

That is why mountain peaks can remain covered with snow even when nearby lowland areas are much warmer.

Higher altitude usually means lower temperatures.

🌊 Oceans Can Warm or Cool Places

Large bodies of water heat up and cool down more slowly than land.

Ocean currents can also transport warm or cold water over enormous distances, affecting the climate of nearby coastal regions.

This is why two places at similar latitudes can sometimes have very different temperatures.

🌬️ Winds Move Heat Around

The atmosphere is constantly moving. Winds transport air from one region to another and help redistribute heat around the planet.

Cold air can move into warmer regions, while warm air can travel toward colder regions.

❄️ Why Are the Poles Extremely Cold?

The Arctic and Antarctic regions receive sunlight at very low angles. During parts of the year, they also experience extremely long periods with little or no direct sunlight.

Snow and ice make things even more interesting because bright surfaces reflect a large amount of incoming sunlight instead of absorbing it.

Less direct sunlight + long winter darkness + reflective ice and snow = extremely cold polar environments.

🏜️ Why Can Deserts Be Cold?

Deserts are not always hot. Some deserts can become surprisingly cold, especially at night or during winter.

Dry air contains little water vapour, and clear skies can allow heat near the ground to escape quickly after sunset.

This can create a huge difference between daytime and nighttime temperatures.

🌎 Location Is Only Part of the Story

Two places at the same latitude do not necessarily have the same climate.

Their temperature can also depend on altitude, nearby oceans, ocean currents, winds, mountains, cloud cover and the type of surface around them.

Latitude tells part of the story — but climate is a combination of many factors.

✨ Quick Facts

☀️ The angle of sunlight strongly affects how much solar energy a region receives.
🏔️ Higher elevations are generally colder than nearby lowlands.
🌊 Oceans and ocean currents help move heat around the planet.

💡 The Big Idea

Some places are cold because they receive less direct solar energy, but that is only the beginning.

Latitude, altitude, oceans, currents, winds, clouds, snow and ice all influence the temperature of a place.

So the next time you see a freezing landscape, remember: temperature is the result of many parts of Earth's climate system working together.
