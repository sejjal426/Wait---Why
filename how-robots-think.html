🤖 How Do Robots Know What to Do?

A robot can move its arm, avoid an obstacle, pick something up, clean a floor, or even navigate through a room. But robots don't magically know what to do. So how do they turn information about the world into actions?

🧠 Robots Have a "Brain"

Robots usually contain a computer or controller that acts as their decision-making system.

This controller receives information, processes it according to its programming or learned models, and sends instructions to the robot's motors and other components.

💡 A simple way to think about it is: Sense → Think → Act.

👀 Step 1: Robots Sense the World

Robots need information about their surroundings before they can react to them.

They can use sensors such as cameras, microphones, distance sensors, touch sensors, temperature sensors and many others.

These sensors convert things happening in the physical world into information that the robot's computer can process.

🧩 Step 2: The Robot Processes the Information

The robot's computer receives the sensor information and processes it.

What happens next depends on what the robot was designed to do.

A simple robot might follow a few fixed rules, while a more advanced robot may use complex software or artificial intelligence to interpret what it senses.

🔍 For example: if a robot detects an object directly in its path, its software may decide that it needs to stop or change direction.

⚙️ Step 3: The Robot Acts

Once the controller decides what should happen, it sends instructions to the robot's actuators.

Actuators are components that create movement or physical action. They can include motors and other mechanisms.

This is how a robot can turn wheels, move an arm, rotate a camera or perform another physical action.

📜 Do Robots Always Follow Instructions?

Some robots follow very specific instructions written by humans.

For example, a factory robot may be programmed to repeatedly perform the same sequence of movements.

Other robots can make choices based on sensor information and software rules rather than following exactly the same movement every time.

The more complicated the environment, the more complicated the robot's decision-making system may need to be.

🤖 What Does AI Add?

Artificial intelligence can allow some robots to perform tasks that require interpreting complex information.

For example, computer vision can help a robot identify objects in camera images.

Machine-learning systems can also help robots learn patterns from data and improve how they perform certain tasks.

🧠 AI doesn't give a robot a human mind. It gives the robot software that can perform particular kinds of intelligent information processing.

🗺️ How Does a Robot Know Where to Go?

Some robots need to understand where they are and where they need to go.

They can use cameras, distance sensors, GPS in suitable environments, maps and other technologies to estimate their position and surroundings.

A navigation system can then help the robot choose a path toward its destination while responding to obstacles.

🔄 Robots Can Continuously Check Their Environment

A robot doesn't always make one decision and then stop thinking.

Many robots repeatedly collect new sensor information and adjust their actions.

Imagine a robot moving toward a destination. If something suddenly appears in front of it, new sensor information can cause the robot to change its movement.

🔄 Sense → Process → Act → Sense again. This loop can happen many times every second.

🧑‍💻 Who Tells the Robot What to Do?

Ultimately, humans design the robot's hardware and software, define its goals and decide what kinds of tasks it should be capable of performing.

Engineers and programmers create the systems that allow the robot to respond to different situations.

In robots that use machine learning, humans also choose training methods and data and evaluate how well the system performs.

✨ Quick Facts

👀 Sensors give robots information about their surroundings.
🧠 Controllers and computers process information and make decisions according to their software.
⚙️ Motors and other actuators allow robots to physically act.

🌌 The Big Idea

Robots don't simply "know" what to do.

They receive information from sensors, process that information using software, and turn the result into physical actions.

Some robots use simple programmed rules, while more advanced systems can use AI and machine learning for tasks that require more complicated decision-making.

So when you see a robot move intelligently, there is usually an entire chain of sensors, software, computers and motors working together behind the scenes. 🤖
