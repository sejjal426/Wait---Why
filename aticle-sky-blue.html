Why is the sky blue?

Look up on a clear day and you'll see a beautiful blue sky. But sunlight actually contains many different colours. So... why does the sky look blue?

🌤️

The short answer

The sky looks blue because Earth's atmosphere scatters sunlight in different directions. Blue light is scattered more strongly than most other visible colours, so our eyes receive blue light from many parts of the sky.

But sunlight looks white. Where does the blue come from?

Sunlight may appear white, but it is actually made up of many colours. You can see these colours when sunlight passes through a prism and forms a rainbow.

When sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere, it interacts with tiny molecules of gases in the air.

Why is blue scattered more?

Blue light has a shorter wavelength than red light. The gases in our atmosphere scatter shorter wavelengths much more strongly.

This scattering sends blue light in many directions. That is why, when we look across the sky, blue light reaches our eyes from all around us.

Then why isn't the sky violet?

Violet light is scattered even more strongly than blue. However, our eyes are more sensitive to blue than violet, and sunlight contains less violet light.

As a result, the sky appears blue to us.

So, next time you look up...

Remember that the blue sky is the result of sunlight interacting with the atmosphere above us.
