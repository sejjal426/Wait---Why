🌙 Why Doesn't the Moon Fall?

Look up at the night sky and you may wonder: if Earth's gravity pulls everything toward it, why doesn't the Moon simply fall down onto Earth?

The answer is fascinating. The Moon is actually falling toward Earth all the time — but it is also moving sideways through space fast enough to keep missing Earth.

🌍 Earth's Gravity Pulls the Moon

Earth has a huge mass, so it produces a strong gravitational force. This force constantly pulls the Moon toward Earth. Without gravity, the Moon would not remain in orbit around our planet.

🚀 So Why Doesn't It Crash?

The Moon is not standing still. It is moving sideways at a very high speed. Because of this sideways motion, while gravity pulls the Moon toward Earth, the Moon keeps moving forward.

Imagine throwing a ball horizontally. It moves forward while Earth's gravity pulls it downward. The Moon works in a similar way, except that it is moving so fast and Earth is curved that it keeps falling around Earth instead of hitting the surface.

🔄 What Is an Orbit?

An orbit is the curved path followed by an object around another object because of gravity. The Moon's orbit is the result of a balance between its forward motion and Earth's gravitational pull.

Gravity continually changes the direction of the Moon's motion, keeping it in orbit rather than allowing it to travel away in a straight line.

🌙 Is the Moon Actually Falling?

Yes! In a sense, the Moon is continuously falling toward Earth. However, because it has enough sideways velocity, it keeps missing Earth. This continuous "falling around Earth" is what we call an orbit.

🧠 A Simple Way to Imagine It

Think of throwing a ball from a very high mountain. If you could throw it fast enough, the Earth's surface would curve away beneath the ball as it falls. The ball could keep going around Earth instead of reaching the ground.

This is essentially the idea behind an orbit. The Moon is constantly being pulled toward Earth, while its forward motion carries it around our planet.

✨ Did You Know?

The Moon takes about 27.3 days to complete one orbit around Earth relative to the distant stars.

🔭 The Big Idea

The Moon doesn't stay in the sky because Earth's gravity stops working. Quite the opposite! Gravity is one of the main reasons the Moon stays in orbit.

The Moon is constantly falling toward Earth, but its sideways motion keeps it moving around Earth instead of crashing into it.
