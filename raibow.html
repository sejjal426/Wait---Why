🌈 How Does a Rainbow Form?

The colourful science hiding inside a tiny raindrop

🌦️ The Big Question

Have you ever looked at the sky after rain and wondered how a beautiful rainbow suddenly appears?

It may look magical, but a rainbow is actually the result of sunlight interacting with tiny water droplets in the atmosphere.

Wait, Why?
Because sunlight enters raindrops, bends, separates into different colours, reflects inside the drops, and then bends again as it comes out.

☀️ Isn't Sunlight Just White?

Sunlight looks white to our eyes, but it is actually made up of many different colours of visible light.

These colours have different wavelengths. When sunlight passes through certain materials, the colours can separate from one another.

A rainbow is one of the most beautiful examples of this separation.

💧 What Happens Inside a Raindrop?

A rainbow is formed through several steps. Imagine sunlight entering a tiny spherical raindrop.

1️⃣ Refraction — Light Bends

When sunlight travels from air into water, its speed changes. Because of this change in speed, the light bends. This bending of light is called refraction.

2️⃣ Dispersion — Colours Separate

The different colours of light bend by slightly different amounts. This causes white sunlight to spread into its different colours. This process is called dispersion.

3️⃣ Internal Reflection

Some of the separated light reaches the inside surface of the raindrop and is reflected back. This is called internal reflection.

4️⃣ Refraction Again

As the light leaves the raindrop and enters the air again, it bends once more. The colours are now separated enough for us to see them.

🌈 Why Do We See Different Colours?

The colours of visible light have different wavelengths, so they are refracted by slightly different amounts when passing through water.

This separates the sunlight into a spectrum of colours.

Red bends the least, while violet bends the most.

This is why the colours appear in a particular order in a primary rainbow:

🔴 Red → 🟠 Orange → 🟡 Yellow → 🟢 Green → 🔵 Blue → 🟣 Indigo → 🟣 Violet

🌧️ Why Do Rainbows Usually Appear After Rain?

After or during rain, there are many tiny water droplets floating in the air.

When sunlight shines through these droplets from the right direction, the droplets act like tiny prisms and separate the sunlight into its different colours.

That is why rain showers combined with sunlight can create rainbows.

👀 Why Can't Everyone See the Same Rainbow?

A rainbow is not a physical object sitting at a particular place in the sky.

You see a rainbow when light reaches your eyes from water droplets at the right angles. If you move, the angle changes too.

This means two people standing in different places can actually see light coming from different groups of water droplets.

✨ Quick “Wait, Why?” Facts

🌈 A rainbow is caused by sunlight and water droplets.

💧 One raindrop can act like a tiny optical system.

☀️ Sunlight contains all the colours of the visible spectrum.

🔬 Refraction, dispersion and internal reflection are important in forming a primary rainbow.

👀 A rainbow's appearance depends on the position of the observer.

🤔 So... Wait, Why?

A rainbow isn't magic. It is nature showing us what sunlight is really made of!

Next time you see one, remember: sunlight + water droplets + the right angle = rainbow 🌈
